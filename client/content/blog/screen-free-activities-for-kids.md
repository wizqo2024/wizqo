## 📵 Phone Away, Learn More: Why Screen‑Free Works
Short, screen‑free activities help kids reset, focus, and build real‑world skills (fine motor, attention, problem‑solving). You don’t need prep time—just a pencil and one printable.

## ⏱️ The 10‑Minute Daily Plan
- 1 min: Pick one printable or quick game
- 7 min: Do the activity (focus on process, not perfection)
- 2 min: Share 1 thing you learned or liked

## ✅ No‑Prep, Screen‑Free Ideas (Pick One Today)
- Dot‑to‑Dot 1–20 (counting + attention)
- Ten‑Frames (1–10) (early number sense)
- Color‑by‑Number (focus + color recognition)
- Spot‑the‑Difference (visual scanning)
- Mini Reading Passage + 3 Qs (comprehension)
- Number Tracing 1–10 (fine motor)

## ❓ FAQs: Screen‑Free at Home (Realistic & Easy)
1. How long should screen‑free time be?
10–15 minutes is enough. Consistency beats length.

2. What if my child resists?
Let them choose the activity. Give a fun reason ("Find 7 differences before snack!").

3. Are printables really helpful?
Yes—fine motor, attention, and early math/reading get a boost without extra setup.

4. How many days per week?
Aim for 3–5 short sessions. Keep it light and fun.

5. Can we mix with light screen time?
Sure—start screen‑free, then optionally add a short online game or video related to the topic.
